# Exercise 9 — Ellipsoid and Its Cross-Sections

## Question

For \(\frac{x^2}{9}+\frac{y^2}{4}+\frac{z^2}{16}=1\), give the semiaxis lengths, describe the section \(z=0\), and describe sections \(z=c\) for \(|c|<4\).

## Solution

The semiaxes along \(x,y,z\) have lengths \(3,2,4\), respectively. At \(z=0\), the cross-section is the ellipse
\[
\frac{x^2}{9}+\frac{y^2}{4}=1.
\]
At \(z=c\), the equation becomes
\[
\frac{x^2}{9}+\frac{y^2}{4}=1-\frac{c^2}{16}.
\]
For \(|c|<4\), this is an ellipse centered at \((0,0,c)\) in the horizontal plane, with semiaxes \(3\sqrt{1-c^2/16}\) and \(2\sqrt{1-c^2/16}\). The sections shrink to a point as \(c\) approaches either endpoint \(4\) or \(-4\).

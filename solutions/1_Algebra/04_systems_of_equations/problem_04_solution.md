# Exercise 4 — Infinitely Many Solutions

The second equation is twice the first. Subtract the third equation from the first:
\(2y=2\), so \(y=1\). The first equation then gives \(x+z=1\).

Let the free variable \(z=s\). Then
\[
(x,y,z)=(1-s,1,s),\qquad s\in\mathbb R.
\]
For \(s=0\), the point \((1,1,0)\) gives \(1+1+0=2\), \(2+2+0=4\), and \(1-1+0=0\), as required.

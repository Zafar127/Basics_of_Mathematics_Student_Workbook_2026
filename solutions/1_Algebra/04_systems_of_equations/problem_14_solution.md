# Exercise 14 — A Small Change in the Right-Hand Side

## Question

Compare the systems
$$\begin{cases}x+y=2,\\2x-y=1,\\3x=3\end{cases}$$
and
$$\begin{cases}x+y=2,\\2x-y=1,\\3x=3.1.\end{cases}$$
Check the consistency of each system and explain why a small change in one number can change the type of the system.

## Solution

Adding the first two equations gives
\[
(x+y)+(2x-y)=2+1,\qquad 3x=3.
\]
Thus \(x=1\), and the first equation gives \(y=1\). The first system's third equation is \(3x=3\), so it is consistent, with unique solution \((1,1)\).

The modified third equation requires \(3x=3.1\), contradicting \(x=1\); the second system has no solution. The first two equations force the left-hand side \(3x\) to equal exactly \(3\), so changing only its required right-hand side makes the system inconsistent.

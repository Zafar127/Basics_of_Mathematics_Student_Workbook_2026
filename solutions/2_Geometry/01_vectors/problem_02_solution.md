# Exercise 2 — Distance as the Length of a Vector

## Question

For \(A=(1,2,3)\) and \(B=(4,-2,6)\), construct \(\overrightarrow{AB}\), compute its length, find the distance using the coordinate formula, and explain why the results agree.

## Solution

The displacement from \(A\) to \(B\) is
\[
\overrightarrow{AB}=B-A=(4-1,-2-2,6-3)=(3,-4,3).
\]
Its length is
\[
\|\overrightarrow{AB}\|=\sqrt{3^2+(-4)^2+3^2}=\sqrt{34}.
\]
The coordinate distance formula gives
\[
d(A,B)=\sqrt{(4-1)^2+(-2-2)^2+(6-3)^2}=\sqrt{34}.
\]
Both calculations use the same coordinate differences \(B-A\); the distance between two points is the length of their displacement vector.

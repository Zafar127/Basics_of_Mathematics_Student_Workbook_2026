# Exercise 7 — Classifying a Second-Degree Equation

## Question

Classify \(4x^2+9y^2=36\), \(x^2-y^2=1\), \(x^2-4y=0\), and \(x^2+y^2+1=0\). Explain why the last equation has no real points.

## Solution

1. Dividing by \(36\) gives \(\frac{x^2}{9}+\frac{y^2}{4}=1\), an ellipse.
2. \(x^2-y^2=1\) is a hyperbola opening in the \(x\)-direction.
3. \(x^2=4y\) is a parabola opening in the positive \(y\)-direction.
4. The equation requires \(x^2+y^2=-1\), impossible over the reals because both squares are nonnegative. It has no real points.

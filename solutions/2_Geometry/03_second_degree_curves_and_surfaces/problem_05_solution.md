# Exercise 5 — Hyperbola and Asymptotes

## Question

For \(\frac{x^2}{16}-\frac{y^2}{9}=1\), find the \(x\)-intercepts and asymptotes, describe a sketch, and explain the far-field role of the asymptotes.

## Solution

Setting \(y=0\) gives \(x^2=16\), so the vertices (and \(x\)-intercepts) are \((\pm4,0)\). The asymptotes are
\[
y=\pm\frac{3}{4}x.
\]
Sketch two branches opening left and right from the vertices and approaching these two lines. Far from the center, the constant \(1\) is small relative to the squared terms, so the equation approaches \(\frac{x^2}{16}-\frac{y^2}{9}=0\), which factors into the two asymptotes.

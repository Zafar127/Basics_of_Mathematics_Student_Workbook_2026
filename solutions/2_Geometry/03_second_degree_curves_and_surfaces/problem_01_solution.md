# Exercise 1 — Recognizing Curves from Canonical Form

## Question

Recognize \(x^2+y^2=9\), \(\frac{x^2}{16}+\frac{y^2}{9}=1\), \(y^2=8x\), and \(\frac{x^2}{9}-\frac{y^2}{4}=1\). Identify their important features and describe how to sketch each.

## Solution

1. \(x^2+y^2=9\) is a circle centered at \((0,0)\), radius \(3\). Sketch a circle through \((\pm3,0),(0,\pm3)\).
2. \(\frac{x^2}{16}+\frac{y^2}{9}=1\) is an ellipse centered at the origin, with semiaxes \(4\) along \(x\) and \(3\) along \(y\). Its vertices are \((\pm4,0)\) and \((0,\pm3)\); draw a horizontally stretched oval through them.
3. \(y^2=8x=4px\) with \(p=2\), so it is a right-opening parabola with vertex \((0,0)\) and focus \((2,0)\). Draw symmetric points about the \(x\)-axis.
4. \(\frac{x^2}{9}-\frac{y^2}{4}=1\) is a hyperbola centered at the origin, opening left and right with vertices \((\pm3,0)\) and asymptotes \(y=\pm\frac23x\).

The sum of two positive squared terms equal to \(1\) identifies a circle or ellipse; a single squared variable equal to a linear term identifies a parabola; and the difference of squared terms identifies a hyperbola.

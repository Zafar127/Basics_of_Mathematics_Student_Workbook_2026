# Exercise 14 — Cone and Cylinder

## Question

Compare \(x^2+y^2=z^2\) and \(x^2+y^2=4\). Describe the sections \(z=c\), \(x=0\), and \(y=0\), and explain how the cross-sectional radii differ.

## Solution

For \(x^2+y^2=z^2\), a horizontal section \(z=c\) is a circle \(x^2+y^2=c^2\), of radius \(|c|\) (a point at \(c=0\)). The sections \(x=0\) and \(y=0\) are \(y^2=z^2\) and \(x^2=z^2\), respectively: pairs of intersecting lines. The surface is a double cone whose radius grows with \(|z|\).

For \(x^2+y^2=4\), every horizontal section is a circle of radius \(2\). The sections \(x=0\) and \(y=0\) are the lines \(y=\pm2\) and \(x=\pm2\), respectively. This is a circular cylinder, with constant radius at every height.

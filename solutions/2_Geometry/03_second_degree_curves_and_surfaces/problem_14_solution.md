# Exercise 14 — Cone and Cylinder

## Question

Compare \(x^2+y^2=z^2\) and \(x^2+y^2=4\). For each surface, describe the sections \(z=c\), \(x=0\), and \(y=0\), make a labelled sketch, and explain why the cross-sectional radii differ.

## Solution

For \(x^2+y^2=z^2\), a horizontal section \(z=c\) is a circle \(x^2+y^2=c^2\), of radius \(|c|\) (a point at \(c=0\)). The sections \(x=0\) and \(y=0\) are \(y^2=z^2\) and \(x^2=z^2\), respectively: pairs of intersecting lines. The surface is a double cone whose radius grows with \(|z|\).

For \(x^2+y^2=4\), every horizontal section is a circle of radius \(2\). The sections \(x=0\) and \(y=0\) are the lines \(y=\pm2\) and \(x=\pm2\), respectively. This is a circular cylinder, with constant radius at every height.

```text
Double cone x²+y²=z²                 Cylinder x²+y²=4
horizontal radius r=|z|              horizontal radius r=2

             z                                  z
             ^                                  ^
            / \                              .-------.
           /   \                             |       |
          /-----\  z=c                       |-------|  z=c
         /       \                            |       |
        /_________\                           .-------.
         \       /                               | |
          \-----/  z=-c                          | |  vertical
           \   /                                 | |  sections
            \ /                                   +-+  are lines
          x²+y²=c²                              x²+y²=4
```

In the cone, each horizontal circle grows with \(|z|\) and the vertical coordinate sections are intersecting lines; in the cylinder, horizontal circles have the same radius \(2\) at every height and vertical coordinate sections are pairs of parallel lines.
